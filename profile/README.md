## 🏆 Meet the Winners

Our three winning teams each tackled a different part of the search-and-rescue challenge.

### 🥇 First Place — Team Shadowcast · $10,000

**Seeing through walls with Wi-Fi.** Shadowcast built a compact prototype that uses existing Wi-Fi signals to detect movement through smoke, darkness, and walls, paired with a visual interface that helps first responders know whether someone is in a room *before* they open the door. Judges were impressed by how tightly the sensing technology was tied to a real decision a responder has to make under danger.

➡️ [**View the Shadowcast repo**](https://github.com/CodeMetalForge-Hackathon/Team-12-Shadowcast)

### 🥈 Second Place — Team DIPCAN · $5,000

**Radio breadcrumbs for the lost.** DIPCAN (Dropped, Indexed, Passive Challenge-Answer Network) created small radio "pucks" an isolated person can leave along their route, letting rescuers reconstruct the trail in Tactical Awareness Kit (TAK) mapping software. Pucks picked up by the wrong person wipe themselves, so the design protects the person leaving the trail as well as the people following it.

➡️ [**View the DIPCAN repo**](https://github.com/CodeMetalForge-Hackathon/Team-21-DIPCAN)

### 🥉 Third Place — Team PULSE · $3,000

**Drone swarms that listen for heartbeats.** PULSE (Profile-driven UAS Localization of Survivors by Echo) built a through-wall heartbeat-detection radar, GPS-free navigation using cameras and motion sensors, and a prototype receiver for satellite-based positioning. Together, these pieces support a drone-swarm concept that steers searches toward the areas where missing people are most likely to be found.

➡️ [**View the PULSE repo**](https://github.com/CodeMetalForge-Hackathon/Team-13-Drone-Search-for-Lost-Subjects)

Congratulations to Shadowcast, DIPCAN, and PULSE, and to every team that shipped a working prototype in a single weekend.

## 🧭 The Challenge

**How can responders locate, identify, and rescue isolated people in dangerous, inaccessible, or rapidly changing environments?**

When infrastructure fails, responders need a faster way to reach people who are cut off, whether that's a hiker lost in the backcountry or a town evacuating ahead of a wildfire. Teams built for degraded, disconnected environments where RF communications, sensing, and resilient information sharing determine the outcome.

| | Objective | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **OBJ.01** | **Locate Isolated People** | Establish position quickly with no reliable GPS, cellular network, or fixed communications. |
| **OBJ.02** | **Detect Life & Hazards** | Search for heat, motion, and sound signatures; identify emerging hazards such as gas or structural risk. |
| **OBJ.03** | **Map Changing Conditions** | Keep a shared operating picture current as fire, weather, water, and terrain evolve. |
| **OBJ.04** | **Deliver Critical Information** | Get actionable information to responders over degraded or congested communications channels. |

**Focus areas:** RF Communications · Sensing · Resilient Info-Sharing · Edge Compute

## ⚖️ How Projects Were Judged

Expert judges from defense, emergency response, academia, and deep tech reviewed every submission on four criteria:

1. **Real-world relevance:** How meaningful is the project to a real problem, user need, or the Forge challenge?
2. **Working live demonstration:** Could the team demonstrate the core capability it built?
3. **Deployability:** Is there a credible path from hackathon demo toward real-world use?
4. **Ideation:** How creative, original, or inventive is the approach?
